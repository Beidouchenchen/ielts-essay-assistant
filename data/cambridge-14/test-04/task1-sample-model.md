## Essay

Grange Park was opened in 1920, in a rectangular area with access from Arnold Avenue at the northern end and Eldon Street to the south. Originally, there were three rose gardens in the grounds, one at each end and one in the centre, with a stage for musicians situated central left, and a glasshouse to the right of the southern entrance. The centre of the park boasted a fountain, with another water feature, a pond for water plants, to the east. Around the edges of the park there were some gardens, with seats nearby for people to sit and enjoy the surroundings.

The park has altered considerably since then. Now there is no large lily-rose garden outside the park, although it does remain on the eastern side, but it is much smaller. One of the smaller rose gardens remains in the north side of the park. The fountain has gone and in its place is a rose garden surrounded by seats. The pond for water plants has been replaced by a water feature, which seems to incorporate some of the plant life previously associated seating. The glasshouse has also gone and in its place is a cafe, with a cafe nearby. Access to the park is still via the two original entrances, but there is now also a further entrance leading to an underground car park.

Despite the apparently significant changes, the park retains the basic elements of flowers, water, seating and music, with the addition of a cafe and a children's play area.
