## Essay

Some people consider that the loss of individual species of plants and animals is the main environmental problem of our time. Others, however, believe that there are other more important environmental issues. I agree with the first viewpoint, and I will outline my reasons below.

In some countries, endangered species are carried out in zoos or other specialist plant and animal facilities, through breeding programmes, preservation orders and other forms of protection and there was genuine success. For example, numbers of golden eagles in the UK were very low in the early twentieth century, but conservation efforts mean that these eagles can now be seen in various parts of the country, including parts of Scotland and Wales.

Survival of the fittest is nature's way of ruthlessly allowing weaker animals to die off, although mankind has added to falling animal numbers through hunting and poaching. However, whether saving individual species should be the main focus of environmental protection is debatable. Climate change and increased use of plastic are having devastating effects on the planet, but their impact is not immediate, so it is easy to disregard them.

The evidence of climate change is there for all to see: rising sea levels and associated flooding with the loss of homes at low land levels are becoming more frequent. Many countries and island communities are now calling for urgent action to delay, halt or even reverse this process, although if we believe the experts, reversing the effects of climate change is virtually impossible. Habitats and ecosystems are gradually being affected by changing temperatures and sea levels, which affects all groups of people, but there seems to be little obvious change taking place. If the major contributors to climate change (China, Russia and the United States) will not support the reduction of greenhouse gases, there is little hope for the future.

Naturalists have also begun to expose the serious problems arising from discarded plastic waste. Birds and animals can die gruesome deaths through being trapped or caught up in plastic bags or ropes, while the long process required for these plastics to decompose means that they remain a danger for many years to come. Perhaps more alarming is the news that marine life is now beginning to ingest small particles of disintegrating plastic, which harms them and all the other creatures in their particular food chain.

The loss of particular species, both flora and fauna, is cause for regret. I don't agree that this is the single most important environmental problem these days. If climate change and damage from plastic increase, there will be less land and less food for everyone, including plants and animals.
