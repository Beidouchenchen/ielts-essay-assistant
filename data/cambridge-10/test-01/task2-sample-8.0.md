# Writing Task 2 Sample Answer
**Source:** Cambridge IELTS 10, Test 1
**Band:** 8.0 (Model Answer)

## Essay
One important step in a child's growth is to develop the ability to tell right from wrong. This skill comes naturally to very few people. Parents and teachers play a crucial role in helping children to learn to tell right from wrong, and most people have much of a role to play in this. Therefore I have to disagree almost entirely with those who think punishment is necessary for this.

To some extent the question depends on the age of the child. To punish a very young child is both wrong and foolish, as an infant will not understand what is happening or why he or she is being scolded. Once the age of reason is reached, however, discipline should be firm but fair. This kind of approach will achieve more than mere punishment, which could amount to physical abuse. It is also, I feel, wrong to single out a child and punish him or her.

To help a child to learn the difference between right and wrong, teachers and parents should firstly provide good role models in their own behaviour. After a child has learnt to distinguish this behaviour from that which is unacceptable, the next stage would be to talk through the reasons. This must be done in a measured, controlled way, as it is easy to damage a child's self-esteem. Furthermore, simply telling a child that his or her behaviour is unacceptable for larger groups must be avoided, as children this age are likely to be self-absorbed and unable to relate to others.

Rather, teachers and parents should use a number of approaches to discipline their young charges, such as detention, withdrawal of privileges, and moral reasoning. Making the child feel that he or she is worth something will also go towards building self-esteem. However, if it is clear that the child has no intention of changing his or her bad behaviour, then it may be necessary to make use of the legal system. This kind of approach should, however, be considered very seriously, as it may result in the development of an unhelpful fear in the child, which leads to much worse future behaviour than does punishment.

## Strengths
- Well-balanced discussion addressing both parts of the question
- Clear position with nuanced arguments
- Effective use of cohesive devices (however, furthermore, rather)

- Complex sentence structures used accurately
- Logical paragraph progression

## Weaknesses
