## Essay

It is often argued that the over 60s are not interested in learning new skills, and that it is not worth teaching them how to use modern technology like smartphones and tablets. While I accept that the older generation may need extra support when learning new skills, I would argue that there are strong reasons for helping them to learn how to use these devices.

First of all, it would be wrong to assume that all older people are technophobic, or lack the mental capacity to use smartphones. Many of them do understand the value of keeping in touch with their family and friends, and see the benefit of using these devices. For example, my grandmother is 75 years old, and she regularly uses WhatsApp to send messages and photos to her grandchildren who live abroad.

Secondly, I think it is important that all people, regardless of their age, should have equal access to technology. In modern society, a great deal of information and services are now available online. For instance, in many countries, it is no longer possible to access government services by phone or in person. If elderly people are unable to use the internet, they may be at a disadvantage when it comes to accessing important services.

In conclusion, while I accept that older people may need more help when learning to use new technology, I believe it is important that they are given the opportunity to do so.

The third paragraph presents the argument about equal access to technology and the changing nature of services, which is also well-supported with a relevant example.

The conclusion summarises the main points and restates the writer's position.
