## Essay

The population in most parts of the world is ageing and living for longer, meaning that there are fewer younger people and more older ones. In some countries, this change is seen as a cause for concern, while in others, the advantages of an ageing population outweigh the disadvantages.

The first reason for concern when considering the ageing population is the expense. If people live longer, for example, a lot more money will need to be spent to support them, particularly with regard to their health. As the body ages, it becomes more fragile and requires more looking after. This will mean that countries will need to find the money to pay for increased levels of care, and that people of working age will have to support more elderly relatives.

That being said, older people are still valuable in terms of their experience and skills. Many people are able to continue working for longer than their parents and grandparents did and, in doing so, they are able to pass on their skills and experience to younger people. In addition, older people who are retired still contribute to the economy in many ways, for instance, by spending money on goods and services and supporting their families and even grandchildren of the working parents.

While an ageing population does pose challenges for governments, it is clear that these are matched by the benefits that having a growing number of elderly people can bring.

## Examiner's Comment

This is a good response to the task. Both sides of the argument are presented, with the case made for both. The candidate makes their position clear in the conclusion.

The second paragraph expresses the drawbacks of ageing on the workforce, the health issues that could be caused to older people, and who should pay for them.

The third paragraph presents the advantages of the wisdom, experience and skills of older people as well as their contribution to the economy and society through supporting their families.

The conclusion summarises the main points and makes it clear that the advantages are more important.

Ideas are relevant, logically organised and there is a clear progression through the response.

A range of linking words and cohesive devices are used, including some referencing [this change | this will mean | these | that having].

The vocabulary is strong, with precise meanings and a range of collocations [ageing population | cause for concern | fragile | pass on their skills | contribute to the economy]. There are some minor errors [care about / look after], but these are minor and do not affect the overall meaning.

There are a range of multi-clause sentences with a variety of structures, including passive constructions, and the response is error free.
